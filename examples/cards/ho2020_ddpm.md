| Term | Definition as used here |

| `beta_t` | Forward-process variance schedule, linear from `beta_1` to `beta_T` (p.5 §4). |
| `L` / `L_simple` | The variational bound, and its unweighted simplification used for training. `L_simple` is the paper's own best-quality setting. |

| `rate / distortion` | Bits per dimension, and RMSE on the [0,255] pixel scale, along the reverse process (p.13 Table 4). |

## §1 One-liner

Trains a diffusion probabilistic model with a reweighted variational bound that equals denoising score matching, and shows it reaches CIFAR10 sample quality competitive with GANs.

## §2 Question

Whether diffusion probabilistic models — a Markov chain that reverses a gradual noising process — can produce **high-quality** samples, which the paper states had not previously been demonstrated, and which parameterisation of the training objective is responsible if they can.

## §3 Prior work

[as-claimed] The paper positions itself against three groups: the original diffusion formulation (Sohl-Dickstein et al.), score-based generative models with annealed Langevin dynamics (NCSN, NCSNv2), and the GAN line (SNGAN, SNGAN-DDLS, BigGAN, StyleGAN2 + ADA). The quantitative claim is Table 1 and nothing else; the card reproduces the rows it relies on rather than paraphrasing them.

Mechanism in one paragraph: a fixed forward chain adds Gaussian noise over `T` steps; a network learns the reverse conditionals `p_theta(x_{t-1} | x_t)`, and the training objective in Eq. (14) is the variational bound with the per-timestep weights dropped, which the paper shows corresponds to denoising score matching. Sampling runs the reverse chain from noise to image, so a single model also yields a rate-distortion curve by stopping the chain early (p.13 Table 4).

## §6 Limitations

### Author-stated

- "Our models do not have competitive log likelihoods compared to other likelihood-based models" (p.2 §1) — the authors state the loss on the likelihood axis themselves.
- The compression reading of the bound is "only a proof of concept ... not yet as a practical compression system" (p.13).
- FID against the training set is 3.17 but against the test set it is 5.24 (p.5 §4.1); the paper reports both rather than only the favourable one.

### Reader-observed

- **"State-of-the-art FID" in the abstract versus its own Table 1.** The abstract (p.1) calls 3.17 "a state-of-the-art FID score"; Table 1 on p.5 lists `Sparse Transformer [7]` at 2.80 in the same unconditional CIFAR10 block, lower than 3.17. The card records both locators and does not decide what qualifier the claim needs — but as printed, the abstract's superlative is not supported by the table beneath it.
- **The FID column mixes two evaluation protocols.** Table 1's own row for this model is a training-set FID (stated in §4.1), while the value the paper supplies for the test-set protocol is 5.24. Whether a given baseline in the same table used train or test is not stated per row, so the ranking in Table 1 is a ranking under an assumption.
- **`IS` carries a standard deviation, `FID` carries none**, on every row of Table 1 and Table 2. The paper's headline improvement (3.17 vs a baseline 3.26) is therefore a comparison of two point estimates with no stated uncertainty.
- **Table 2's blanks are explained in the caption, not in the table body.** "Blank entries were unstable to train and generated poor samples with out-of-range scores" — the empty cells are negative results, and a reader who skips the caption reads them as missing data.
- **The 0.00000 rate at 100 steps is a printed value, not an exact zero.** It is reported to five decimals; treating it as exactly zero would make a ratio involving it undefined.

## §7 Transferable

- **Sweep one axis per row and leave the failures visible.** Table 2's blanks, plus the caption that explains them, record attempts that did not train. That is cheap to copy and it is the opposite of dropping failed runs silently.
- **Report the metric under both evaluation protocols when they differ.** The train-set 3.17 and test-set 5.24 pair is a model for how to publish a headline number without hiding the alternative reading.
- **A rate-distortion table turns "the model can decode progressively" from a claim into a table.** Two columns, ten rows, no extra experiment beyond sampling at truncated chain lengths.
- **Approximate parameter count and wall-clock per stage belong in the appendix**, in prose if there is no table: 35.7M parameters, 21 steps/s, 10.6 hours. All three are what a reproduction attempt needs first.
